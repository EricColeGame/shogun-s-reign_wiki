## Economy and Ryō: Early Labor and Career Paths

The primary currency of the realm is **Ryō**, and newly spawned characters arrive with virtually none. Your immediate priority in any **shogun s reign tutorial** walkthrough is establishing a predictable earning loop. Without Ryō, you cannot purchase merchant inventory, claim housing, or acquire better equipment.

Community reports indicate that exact wage formulas and NPC vendor rates are adjusted periodically by developers, but the foundational loops remain consistent. The most reliable day-one income streams come from manual labor and trade.

| Starter Role | Primary Gameplay Loop | Earning Potential | Ideal Player Type |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Farmer** | Tend crop fields, harvest produce, sell raw yields | Steady, low risk | Beginners who want predictable solo income |
| **Merchant** | Buy bulk goods (turnips, cabbage), sell at market stalls | High ceiling, requires capital | Players who enjoy trading, haggling, and managing stock |
| **Attendant** | Assist patrons in bathhouses, perform service duties | Moderate, tip-reliant | Social roleplayers who value atmospheric immersion |
| **Performer** | Play musical rhythm mini-games for audiences | Skill-dependent | Players with good timing seeking performance badges |

### The Farming Routine
For complete novices, the Farmer role provides the safest entry point. You do not need upfront capital to begin working the plots. By harvesting crops and delivering them to collection points, you build your initial reserve of Ryō without risking financial loss. 

### The Merchant Trading Cycle
Once you save a modest sum of Ryō through farming, transitioning to a Merchant role unlocks higher profit margins. You purchase wholesale agricultural goods—most commonly staple vegetables like turnips and cabbage—and set up shop at an available market stall. Success as a merchant requires active engagement: call out your wares to passing travelers, negotiate fair prices, and keep your stall stocked during peak server hours.

## Social Etiquette and Edo Roleplay Grammar

Unlike standard action experiences on the platform, this world operates heavily on roleplay etiquette. Treating the server as an open battleground will quickly earn your character a poor reputation among town guards and nobles. Understanding social grammar is a core requirement of this **shogun s reign tutorial**.

```
[Arrive in Village] ──> [Observe Local Customs] ──> [Respect Indoor Rules] ──> [Earn Social Standing]
```

Respecting physical spaces is the first rule of survival. Indoor locations, particularly teahouses, residential estates, and public bathhouses, require specific behaviors:

*   **Footwear Removal:** Always remove sandals or geta before stepping onto tatami mats or entering clean communal bathhouse floors.
*   **Deference to Titles:** Characters holding formal retainer, samurai, or ministerial titles expect polite greetings. Bowing before addressing superiors prevents conflict.
*   **Scene Integrity:** Avoid interrupting ongoing dramatic exchanges or private negotiations with erratic jumping or out-of-character chat messages.
*   **Combat Discipline:** Keep weapons sheathed unless an organized conflict or formal duel has been mutually acknowledged.

| Location | Expected Etiquette | Common Mistake to Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bathhouse** | Remove footwear; maintain respectful, quiet interaction | Running through pools or initiating aggressive confrontations |
| **Marketplace** | Form orderly queues; respect stall claims | Standing directly on top of vendor tables or spamming chat |
| **Noble Estates** | Await permission to enter; address guards courteously | Trespassing into private chambers without an escort |
| **Farming Fields** | Respect occupied plots; avoid trampling crops | Stealing harvest nodes while another player is working |

Adhering to these community norms ensures that established factions, guilds, and retainers welcome you into their inner circles, opening doors to advanced progression routes.
