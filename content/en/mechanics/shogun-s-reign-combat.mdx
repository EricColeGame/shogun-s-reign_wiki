## Core Foundations: Posture, Stamina, and Guard Mechanics

At its heart, **Shogun's Reign combat** relies on a dual-resource framework: Stamina and Posture. Stamina fuels your offensive repertoire—governing light slashes, heavy thrusts, dashes, and directional rolls. If your stamina hits zero, your warrior enters a winded state, disabling defensive maneuvers and drastically slowing your movement speed.

Posture, by contrast, represents balance and physical composure under fire. Blocking attacks drains posture rather than raw health. When an incoming hit pushes your posture gauge past its absolute limit, your guard breaks. A guard break leaves you stunned for several critical moments, giving your opponent a guaranteed window for an unblockable execution or combo sequence.

```
Incoming Attack ──► Block / Guard ──► Posture Depleted? ──► [YES] Guard Break / Heavy Stun
                                          │
                                         [NO]
                                          ▼
                                   Retain Balance
```

Managing these twin meters requires careful observation. Backpedaling without blocking speeds up your posture recovery, while holding your defensive guard slows stamina regeneration down to a crawl. Balancing offense and retreat is the true benchmark of elite swordsmanship.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Guard** | None (Passive drain per hit) | Moderate build-up per deflected blow | Very slow while held | Low (vulnerable to guard breaks) |
| **Perfect Parry** | Minimal upfront cost | Zero posture damage (damages attacker's posture) | Instant recovery to neutral | High (strict timing window) |

### Light Attacks vs. Heavy Variations

In standard duels, light attacks serve as hit-confirms. They chain together quickly and can be canceled into defensive postures during early startup frames. However, repeated light attacks are easy to read and invite devastating parry counters.

Heavy attacks bypass standard guard efficiency and inflict heavy posture trauma. Charging a heavy attack alters release timing, confusing opponents who preemptively raise their guard. Integrating delayed heavy strikes into your normal strings remains an essential tactic within **Shogun's Reign combat**.

## Precision Timing: Master Parries, Feints, and Counters

Defensive mastery separates casual players from tournament-caliber duelists. The parry system operates on narrow active frames, typically triggering within the first fraction of a second after raising your guard. 

If your parry connects with an enemy swing, your opponent suffers an immediate recoil animation, resetting their combo and opening them to instant retribution. Missing the parry window, however, forces your guard into a standard block or outright leaves you open to clean damage.

To avoid becoming predictable, skilled warriors employ feints. By canceling an attack animation midway through its startup, you force your adversary to commit to a parry or dodge early. Once their defensive window lapses during the recovery phase, you unleash your true assault.

## Tactical Footwork, Spacing, and Whiff Punishing

Positioning is just as important as blade speed. Whiff punishing—the art of baiting an enemy into swinging at empty air, then punishing their recovery frames—is the cleanest way to deal unanswerable damage in **Shogun's Reign combat**.

To execute consistent whiff punishes, keep your warrior hovering just outside the strike radius of your foe's blade. The moment they commit to an animation that misses, step inward to exploit their open frames.

```
Opponent Swings (Out of Range) ──► Whiff Occurs ──► You Close Distance ──► Guaranteed Counter
```

Sprinting drains stamina, so seasoned duelists favor rhythmic walking and micro-dashes. Walking preserves your meter, ensuring full access to evasive rolls whenever an unblockable thrust enters your space.

## Common Mistakes and How to Exploit Enemy Habits

According to widespread community reports, novice duelists frequently tap their block button repeatedly in hopes of securing a lucky parry. This practice, known as "parry fluttering," actually increases vulnerability by shrinking active parry windows with each rapid press. When you notice an opponent fluttering their guard, delay your strike by half a second to land a clean blow right as their active frames collapse.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What causes an instant guard break in Shogun's Reign combat?
An instant guard break occurs when an incoming strike depletes the remainder of your posture meter while you are actively blocking. Certain heavy weapons, such as the Kanabo or fully charged two-handed strikes, deal massive posture trauma that can shatter your guard in just one or two deflected blows if your posture is already compromised.

### How can I improve my parry timing in Shogun's Reign combat?
Improving your parry consistency requires studying the startup animations of your opponent's weapon rather than watching their character model as a whole. Activate your parry right as the enemy blade transitions from the windup phase to its forward swing. Avoid spamming the block button, as repeated inputs diminish the active parry window.

### Does armor weight affect my combat mobility?
Yes. Based on player experience and mechanical testing, equipping heavier armor sets increases your passive damage reduction and resistance to stagger at the expense of stamina regeneration and roll distance. Lighter armor configurations allow for rapid repositioning, making them ideal for agile dueling styles.

### Is spacing more important than combo length in Shogun's Reign combat?
Spacing is widely considered more vital than long combos because extended attack chains can be parried or interrupted. Proper spacing allows you to bait enemy whiffs and execute clean, risk-free punishes while keeping your own resources safe.
